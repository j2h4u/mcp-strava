# Cardiac Drift: Steady-State Requirement

_For athletes, coaches, and developers: why decoupling (Pa:Hr) requires constant pace, and why this athlete's variable-pace training makes it N/A. A distinct metric from cardiac cost._

## Definition (all sources unanimous)

Cardiac drift (cardiovascular drift, Pa:Hr decoupling) is HR increase during **prolonged exercise at CONSTANT intensity/pace**. Without steady-state, the metric measures something else entirely — pace change, not cardiovascular drift.

## Key Sources

### Wikipedia — Cardiovascular Drift
> "Cardiovascular drift is characterized by decreases in mean arterial pressure and stroke volume and a parallel increase in heart rate" during **steady-state exercise** without an increase in workload. Onset after 5-10 min in warm environments.

### Uphill Athlete — Heart Rate Drift Test
> "The test involves **maintaining a steady effort or pace** while recording your heart rate over a 60-minute period." Explicitly: "maintain **absolutely constant pace and incline** (do not speed up or slow down)."

### Marathon Handbook — Cardiac Drift
> "Cardiac drift refers to the upward rise of your heart rate during exercise over a long, continuous workout, **even though the intensity of exercise has remained constant**."

### Runners Connect
> "Cardiovascular drift refers to the natural increase in heart rate that occurs when running with **little or no change in pace**."

### TrainingPeaks Pa:Hr (decoupling)
Formula: `(Avg HR second half / Avg HR first half) / (Avg pace second half / Avg pace first half) − 1`

This formula only makes sense when pace₁ ≈ pace₂. If pace changes structurally, the denominator shift dominates the result.

## Physiological Mechanism

1. Core body temperature ↑ → blood shunted to skin for cooling
2. Sweating → plasma volume ↓ → stroke volume ↓
3. Heart rate ↑ to maintain cardiac output despite lower stroke volume
4. Result: same effort, higher HR

This cascade requires SUSTAINED effort. If you walk instead of run, you're reducing effort — different physiology entirely.

## Why It Fails for Variable-Pace Activities

| Scenario | First Half | Second Half | What Decoupling Measures |
|----------|-----------|-------------|--------------------------|
| Steady run | 145 bpm @ 9 km/h | 155 bpm @ 9 km/h | True cardiac drift (+7%) |
| Run then walk | 145 bpm @ 9 km/h | 120 bpm @ 5 km/h | Pace change, NOT drift |
| Mountain hike | 130 bpm @ 3 km/h (up) | 110 bpm @ 5 km/h (down) | Terrain change, NOT drift |

## This Athlete's Data (May 2026)

7 recent Night Runs: CV 32-50% (all > 25% threshold)
8 recent Hikes: CV 31-51% (all > 25% threshold)

Conclusion: no valid steady-state activities exist in recent history. Decoupling is effectively N/A for this athlete's training pattern.
